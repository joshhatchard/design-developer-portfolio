WHAT IS A BUS?

DESIGN AND FORM

The 1963 Volkswagen Kombi is a boxy, rear-engine bus with a rounded front and large windshield. Its simple, functional design emphasises practicality and approachability.Inspired by Dutch importer Ben Pon’s sketch, it prioritises simplicity, economy and versatility.

HISTORY AND CULTURE

Part of the first generation “split-window” series (1950–1967), the 1963 model reflects both postwar efficiency and early 1960s youth culture. Influenced by modernist design principles, economic constraints and growing recreational lifestyles it balanced practicality with freedom of use.

INFLUENCE AND SYMBOLISM

Used in Germany for work and across the world by surfers, hippies and artists, it became both a vehicle and a cultural symbol. Functionally, it transports people and goods. Culturally, it is often considered a bus because of its communal use and symbolic role in mobility, creativity and social freedom.

MISSED THE BUS?

CRITICAL ANALYSIS

The 1963 VW Kombi, once a practical vehicle, has evolved into a nostalgic icon. Once symbolizing freedom and mobility, it’s now valued more for its vintage charm and cultural significance than its function.

TRANSFORMATION

In my redesign, I transformed the Kombi into a bus stop, subverting its purpose from movement to waiting. Fragmented through scattered elements and enhanced with playful interactions like swings and graffiti, it becomes a space for interaction. Both a structure and a statement.

RATIONALE

This reconfiguration highlights the irony between public art and infrastructure. Cities often favour symbolic beautification over fixing issues like unreliable transport. By turning a bus into a bus stop, the work mirrors this dysfunction and uses art to expose how aesthetics can mask systemic problems. The challenge lay in balancing visual disruption with conceptual clarity inviting reflection on the form, function and civic irony.
