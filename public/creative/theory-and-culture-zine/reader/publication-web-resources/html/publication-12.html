CAN YOU BUY ADVENTURE?

SECOND ORDER OF DESIGN - CONSUMERISM

CRITICAL ANALYSIS

The 1963 VW Kombi, once a symbol of freedom, adventure, and communal living embodied a lifestyle separate from materialism. Over time it shifted from a living emblem of independence to a nostalgic collectible, absorbed by the very consumer culture it once differed from.

TRANSFORMATION

In my redesign, I turned the Kombi into a symbol of hyper-consumerism, it’s covered in ads and logos to mirror today’s marketing overload. Moss overtakes its roof hinting at decay and neglect. Inside, two opposing spaces appear, one worn and practical, the other luxurious yet deteriorating. A reflection of excess built on limiting cycles.

RATIONALE

This transformation critiques how consumerism distorts value and purpose. The Kombi’s evolution from tool to trophy reflects how society commodifies authenticity and experience. Its divided interior underscores class divides in what endures versus what decays. The challenge lay in balancing irony with realism, revealing the fragility and facade of consumption-driven identity.

WHY LEAVE HOME?

THIRD ORDER OF DESIGN - SERVICE DESIGN

CRITICAL ANALYSIS

The 1963 VW Kombi was valued for its simplicity and utility, offering reliable transportation. Its appeal lay in function over form, a practical vessel for adventure rather than an aestheticised experience.

TRANSFORMATION

In my redesign, the Kombi becomes a mobile service hub. Instead of carrying passengers to destinations, it moves through the city as robotic arms deliver goods and services directly to user. Its adaptable interior supports consumption, interaction and comfort, transforming the vehicle into a moving node of convenience.

RATIONALE

This transformation critiques how service design prioritises seamlessness over substance. By literalising convenience, it exposes the tendency to aestheticise problem-solving while overlooking deeper social and infrastructural issues. The challenge was creating a system both believable and responsive. The result mirrors elegance yet questions whether modern solutions genuinely confront underlying complexities.
