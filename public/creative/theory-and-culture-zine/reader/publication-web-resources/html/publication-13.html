GREEN ENOUGH?

FOURTH ORDER OF DESIGN - CIRCULAR DESIGN

CRITICAL ANALYSIS

The 1963 VW Kombi predates circular design but naturally reflects its principles through modularity, durability and reparability. Its simple construction and longevity made it a resilient, long-lasting vehicle even though it was never intended with sustainability or closed-loop systems in mind.

TRANSFORMATION

In my redesign, the Kombi is cloaked in trees, vines and bushes creating a facade of eco-friendliness. Behind the greenery, it emits fumes and leaks oil, contradicting its green imagery. This contrast exposes the gap between perception and reality, highlighting a product that appears sustainable while ignoring true circular design principles.

RATIONALE

The work critiques “greenwashing” in design highlighting products marketed as eco-friendly without real sustainability. By exaggerating the gap between appearance and function it exposes the disconnect between corporate messaging and environmental reality. The challenge lay in balancing irony with visual cohesion to offer a thought provoking commentary on authenticity versus image in contemporary design.

WHO REALLY TRAVELS?

FIFTH ORDER OF DESIGN - POSTHUMAN DESIGN

CRITICAL ANALYSIS

The 1963 VW Kombi is a classic vehicle known for its versatile design and cultural significance. Centred on human needs, mobility, practicality and social experience it predates posthuman design thinking, which integrates humans and non-human systems.

TRANSFORMATION

In my redesign, the Kombi keeps its iconic look but is outfitted with posthuman technology. It navigates diverse environments and interacts with both living and non-living systems acting as a node within a broader network. Yet human bias persists, windows and interfaces remain tailored to human perception, highlighting our everlasting influence.

RATIONALE

This design examines the tension between posthuman ideals and human centred bias. By creating a vehicle that engages multiple systems yet remains human-oriented, it critiques the limits of universal design. The challenge was balancing futuristic function with familiar scale. The piece shows that even abstract frameworks often still prioritise humanity and its needs.
