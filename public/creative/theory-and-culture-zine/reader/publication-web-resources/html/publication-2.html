PUBLIC ART

AND ENGAGEMENT

INTRODUCTION

Public Art serves as a tool for placemaking and urban renewal, it uses creative interventions in public spaces to enhance community interaction. Matthews and Gadaloff (2022) describe it as a means to strengthen social cohesion, civic identity and participation through tangible and visible artistic outputs that engage the public.

DESIGNER

Grimanesa Amoros is a urban public artist renowned for her immersive light-based installations. In 2011, she created Uros House for New York City‘s Times Square, inspired by the floating dwellings of Peru‘s Uros people. This site specific work utilized LED lighting and translucent materials to explore themes of cultural identity and adaptability in urban environments.

CRITIQUE

While public art can enrich spaces and foster social connection, it sometimes prioritises aesthetic impact over practical function which can overshadow usability in design. Its emphasis on placemaking can risk homogenising urban experiences, suggesting that designers must carefully balance creative expression with the everyday needs of the community.
