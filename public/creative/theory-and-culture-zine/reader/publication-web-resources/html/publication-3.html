MATERIAL CULTURE

INTRODUCTION

Material Culture focuses on designed objects as expressions of societal values, technologies and aesthetics. Csikszentmihalyi (1991) highlights how objects convey meaning beyond function and reflect the cultural and historical context in which they are created.

DESIGNER

Dutch designer Hella Jongerius explores material culture through her innovative use of colour, texture and craft in industrial design. She blends traditional handcraft with modern production to question consumerism and authenticity. Her “Polder Sofa” for Vitra exemplifies this by combining diverse fabrics, colours and buttons to celebrate material diversity and imperfection.

CRITIQUE

While material culture enriches design by embedding meaning and reflecting social values, it can limit innovation by prioritising tradition over practicality or adaptability. Designers may focus on symbolic significance at the expense of functionality or sustainability and risk designs that honour heritage but fail to meet contemporary user needs effectively.
